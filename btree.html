Independent systems-software project

A disk-backed B-tree with explicit durability and correctness checks.

I built a Rust key-value engine to understand the invariants beneath a database API: fixed page formats, bounded caching, crash recovery, deletion rebalancing, and operation-by-operation comparison with SQLite.

Mutation and recovery

Correctness depends on preserving both tree invariants and the ordering of durable writes.

1 · Rebalance

Split full children before insertion and borrow or merge before deletion descent.

2 · Record

Collect complete after-images for every page changed by the public mutation.

3 · Protect

Write and synchronize a checksummed redo record before checkpointing dirty pages.

4 · Recover

Replay a complete record after interruption, then clear the log only after a durable checkpoint.

Correctness strategy

The engine is tested from both sides: local invariants inside the page and tree code, and observable behavior against a mature database.

Generated operation traces

Property tests generate sequences of inserts, replacements, deletes, and reads. The engine must preserve ordering, free-page reuse, range boundaries, and parent-child relationships throughout the trace.

SQLite as an oracle

The same operation stream is applied to an in-memory SQLite table. Point lookups, deletion results, and complete range output are compared after each relevant step so structural bugs become externally visible.

Engineering decisions

The implementation favors inspectable constraints over broad database features.

Deliberate limits

  • Signed 64-bit keys and bounded byte-string values
  • A 64-page least-recently-used cache
  • One writer and an explicit close/checkpoint boundary
  • Range scans over persistent leaf and internal records

Failure surfaces made visible

  • Corrupt page identities and invalid metadata
  • Truncated or checksummed log records
  • Separator errors during borrow and merge
  • Free-list persistence across reopen
