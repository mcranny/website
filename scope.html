Independent instrumentation project

Modeling the complete path from waveform to scope measurement.

I built a virtual oscilloscope to explore how signal generation, converter behavior, analog response, sampling, triggering, and measurement algorithms combine to produce the trace an engineer actually sees.

Acquisition result

The capture artifact exposes both the waveform and the measurements derived from the full-resolution record.

Modeled signal chain

Each stage is isolated so a test can change one impairment without obscuring where the resulting measurement error came from.

1 · Waveform

Create deterministic sine, square, and other source signals at controlled sample rates.

2 · Conversion

Apply gain, offset, quantization, effective-number-of-bits noise, and optional nonlinearity.

3 · Analog path

Model noise, drift, attenuation, bandwidth limits, and fractional-sample delay.

4 · Acquisition

Apply sampling, aperture jitter, triggering, record history, and measurement extraction.

Measurement correctness

The simulator keeps display data separate from measurement data so visualization decimation cannot silently change the result.

What I validate

  • FFT-based frequency placement and reconstruction behavior
  • Gain, offset, quantization, noise, and bandwidth impairments
  • Trigger edge, level, pre-trigger fraction, and acquisition history
  • Frequency and peak-to-peak measurements on full records

Why the boundary matters

A browser can draw a compact trace while the measurement engine continues to use the complete capture. That separation mirrors a common instrumentation concern: presentation should not alter the data used for engineering decisions.

Interactive result

A small browser application exposes waveform, analog-path, and scope controls while a dedicated acquisition endpoint returns trace and measurement data.

Operator controls

The viewer provides timebase, trigger, waveform, conversion, and analog-path parameters in a scope-like interface.

Application boundary

The simulation engine remains independent of the browser. That keeps the numerical model testable and lets other clients request acquisitions without coupling measurement logic to the UI.

Boundary: This independent simulator is intended for software validation and engineering exploration. Its converter and analog models are explicit approximations rather than calibrated replacements for physical lab instruments.
